// Roadmap

From foundations to Swiss VAT.

Kesh is delivered as a sequence of epics, each composed of small, independently reviewable stories. The release plan below tracks the path from MVP (v0.1) through sovereignty tooling (v0.2), Swiss VAT (v0.3), supplier invoices, payments & project analytics (v0.4), structured QR-bill addresses (v0.5), invoice emailing with multilingual templates (v0.6), receivables & multi-level dunning (v0.7), year-end closing with automatic balance carry-forward (v0.8), advanced invoicing (v0.9), duplicate prevention (v0.10), translation debt (v0.11), the correction wave that made the books right (v0.12), open items & matching (v0.13), and supplier invoices from the document to the payment (v0.14, current release) — toward a feature-complete production release. Kesh is not yet meant for keeping real books: no year-end closing has yet been held in Kesh — the closing checks (v0.13.4) and the reversal of accruals (v0.14.0) have shipped, a dry run of a closing is still to be written — and supporting documents on journal entries (#235) and in the installation backup (#503) are still missing.

E17 — Infrastructure & Sovereignty v0.2.0

Done

External API with personal access tokens (read / read-write, per company), full installation export/import (.keshbackup) for SSH-free migration and restore, self-service password recovery by email (anti-enumeration, single-use 30-min links), atomic first-admin setup (TOCTOU fix).

E11 / E18 — Swiss VAT & VAT accounting v0.3.0

Done

Per-period VAT computation and report, multiple rates (standard, reduced, accommodation); output VAT posted automatically when sales invoices are validated; assisted entry of purchases with input VAT; VAT return (net amount due to the FTA) and reconciliation of the report against the general ledger. Official FTA e-decompte (ESTV) format planned for a later release.

E12 — Credit notes, supplier invoices & payments v0.3.2 / v0.4.0

Done

Credit notes (linked reversal of a validated invoice) — shipped in v0.3.2. Supplier invoices with binary settlement, ISO 20022 pain.001.001.09 outgoing payment file for Swiss e-banking, and folder-based supplier-invoice import with server-side Swiss QR-Bill decoding — shipped in v0.4.0.

E19 — Project cost accounting (analytics) v0.4.0

Done

An optional project dimension (two levels) taggable across every entry flow — manual journal entries (per line), sales & supplier invoices (per document), and bank reconciliation (match, split, rule default). Two exportable reports (PDF/CSV): Expenses by project (all charges, drill-down to the entry, for tax deductions) and Return by project (invested cost / revenue / net result / yield %), with sub-project roll-up and fiscal-year or cumulative views.

E20 — Invoice emailing & business email templates v0.6.0

Done

Send a validated invoice by email in one click — Swiss QR-Bill PDF attached, subject and body pre-filled in the contact's language with a personalised salutation, recipient locked to the contact card (anti-spoofing), reply-to set to your company mailbox. Admin-editable multilingual templates (FR/DE/IT/EN) with substitution variables and sensible zero-config defaults; per-contact correspondence language and salutation; every send recorded in the audit log, and resend support.

E21 — Receivables & dunning v0.7.0

Done

The full debtor follow-up cycle: structured payment terms on the contact (auto-calculated invoice due dates), correct VAT-inclusive amounts everywhere a total is shown (QR-Bill, PDF, email, receivables list), an aged-receivables balance of open items, and multi-level payment reminders — configurable levels & fees, an auto-populated list of invoices to chase, manual sending by email through the multilingual templates (E20), and a per-invoice reminder history.

E14 — Year-end closing & balance carry-forward v0.8.0

Done

Balance-sheet figures now carry forward automatically from one fiscal year to the next, computed in real time from inception — no closing or carry-forward entries to post by hand. The balance sheet shows retained earnings and result for the year as distinct lines inside a dedicated equity section (CO art. 959a). Closing a year is a lock (immutability + audit, CO art. 957-964), and an administrator can reopen it against a recorded justification. Every account carries an explicit role — Kesh never infers an account’s purpose from its number, so you can renumber your chart of accounts freely. Opening balances let you migrate an existing set of books in one balanced entry.

E16 — Advanced invoicing v0.9.0

Done

Each invoice line can now carry its own revenue account, falling back to the company default — the accounting entry splits the credit across the right accounts, credit notes included. Each catalogue item can hold a default revenue account that pre-fills the line. The invoice PDF is complete at last: a VAT recap by rate, your own contact details under your address, and the customer number you assign to a contact — unique per company, searchable in the address book, and printed on credit notes too.

E22 — Duplicate prevention & hardening v0.10.0

Done

As you type a new contact, Kesh warns you that a similar one may already exist — case, accents, hyphens and invisible characters are all ignored when comparing — and flags a VAT number (IDE) already taken, including by an archived contact, the one case where saving would be refused by a record you cannot see. It warns, it never blocks: two clients may legitimately share a near-identical name. Also in this release: canonical customer numbers — two numbers identical to the eye are now the same number, whatever the Unicode composition or database collation; hardened API keys — a PAT can no longer reach any administration route, so revoking a compromised key is once again enough; and an integration test suite 42× faster.

E23 — Translation debt v0.11.0

Done

Kesh advertises four languages, and on part of its screens it was serving French to everyone — not raw keys or error messages, but correct French, indistinguishable from a deliberate translation, with every check green. A missing label simply fell back to the French text embedded in the code. 1600 messages have been written: 358 new French labels and 414 in each of the three other languages, so all four catalogues now hold the same 1630 keys. Six guards keep the gap from reopening silently — a label requested but missing, a language losing a key the others have, text hard-coded past the translation layer, one label drifting into two meanings — each proven by recreating the very defect it claims to catch. Translations have not been reviewed by native speakers: a glossary fixes the Swiss accounting terminology and systematic checks ruled out clashes of meaning, but nothing guarantees the language sounds natural.

E24 — Getting the books right v0.12.0

Done

A review of the whole product by three experts — accountant, tax adviser, finance director — found the founding defect: collecting a customer payment wrote no accounting entry, while the balance sheet stayed balanced and no check turned red. This wave adds the payment entry, the general ledger, reversal of an entry — correcting without erasing, the original left intact (CO art. 958f) —, and a period lock so that a quarter already declared no longer moves.

E25 — The correction wave, completed v0.12.1

Done

The Wave 1 milestone closed at zero open issues: an audit trail that holds and can be read on screen; entries never renumbered; a validated invoice un-validated instead of deleted, and payments, reconciliations and supplier payments cancelled by reversal; the amount still due right everywhere, including write-offs; a trial balance that agrees with the balance sheet; and a sovereignty export that carries the whole of the books.

E13 — Budgets

Annual budgets per account, variance reports, cash-flow forecasting.

E15 — Open items & matching, receipts, custom journals v0.13.0

Matching released — epic open

Released in v0.13.0: the open items of a balance-sheet account at any date — what remains open, line by line, totalling the account balance; manual matching with suggested pairs — Kesh suggests, you confirm, it never matches a suggestion on its own — and unmatching; a settled customer invoice and a paid supplier invoice are matched with their payments, a reversal with the entry it corrects, existing data included at upgrade; the matching code shown on journal entries and in the general ledger. Also: an entry can be edited or deleted while its fiscal year is open, fiscal years close in order, and the .env configuration finally reaches Kesh. ⚠️ No way back to v0.12.x once the database is upgraded — back it up first. Still to come: document attachments, versioned parsers and templates, user-definable journal types.

E26 — Acceptance testing of v0.13.0 v0.13.1

Released

Found by importing a real statement: in debit/credit CSV profiles, every UBS expense became a receipt, silently. The sign of debits is now a profile setting, never a guess, and a statement whose amount contradicts its column is refused as a whole; up to 20 header lines let the UBS CSV import as it is. Bank accounts get an optional short description (“UBS — Personal”) shown in every list and picker; the Synology restore script fails closed while Kesh may be running; the manual describes the actual chart-of-accounts and import screens. ⚠️ No way back to v0.13.0 once the database is upgraded — back it up first.

E27 — Acceptance testing of v0.13.1 v0.13.2

Released

A bank import none of whose transactions is reconciled can now be deleted, then re-imported — traced in the audit log. The currency of a statement is read — an optional Currency column in CSV profiles, the amount’s own currency in CAMT.053 — and a statement not in Swiss francs is refused instead of being booked in CHF without a word. Changing the profile after the preview recomputes it, and an import whose profile, account or file changed since the preview is refused. A reconciliation batch holding a transaction that has disappeared is processed proposal by proposal instead of failing as a whole. No database migration: going back to v0.13.1 remains possible once the Currency column is removed from profiles.

E28 — Six bugs and closing a bank account v0.13.3

Released

A bank account can now be closed, and reopened: it becomes read-only, its history stays whole, and nothing more can be booked on it. A rejected bank transaction can be put back among the reconciliation proposals from its import’s detail. The installation backup reads all its tables in a single consistent snapshot. Reconciliation no longer proposes an invoice it would then refuse, and a debit offered against a customer invoice is refused cleanly. An error is never displayed as “[object Object]” any more, and destructive buttons are visible again. One database migration, which does not raise the minimum version — but going back to v0.13.2 requires restoring the backup taken before the upgrade.

E29 — Preparing the first year-end closing, part one v0.13.4

Released

Preparing a fiscal year’s closing: seven read-only checks — bank transactions still to reconcile, missing year-end statement, draft invoices, imported bills to complete, inconsistent entries — each leading to the screen where to act. A bank transaction can be linked to the payment already recorded on the invoice, without a new entry. Reconciliation now proposes a payment received after the due date or a reminder, puts the invoice that fell due first at the top on equal score (art. 87 CO), no longer proposes an invoice a payment exceeds by a few cents, and says which proposal it shows. An import, a payment batch or a cancellation started just before a bank account is closed no longer lands on it. The reconciliation list says when older transactions remain beyond the 100 most recent. No database migration: going back to v0.13.3 only takes its image. Automatic reversal of accruals at the opening of the next year follows in v0.14.0.

E29 — Preparing the first year-end closing, part two v0.14.0

Released

Accruals and deferrals reversed on the first day of the next fiscal year, from the entry itself (#232): a prepaid expense, accrued income or an invoice still to receive is booked on the last day of the year, and its reversal is dated the first day of the next one, in that year. A dry run of a closing in the administration manual is moved to E31, after the complete backup.

E30 — Supplier invoices, from the document to the payment v0.14.0

Released

Every supplier document, with or without a QR code, enters a “Documents to process” list with a preview; the bill is created next to its document, the supplier proposed — never chosen automatically — or created without leaving the form (#685). A document can be attached to a bill afterwards, detached, and viewed from the journal entry. Reconciliation proposes the open supplier bill for a debit and settles it; cancelling the match undoes the payment (#686). The same bill can no longer enter two pending payment batches. One breaking database migration: going back to v0.13.4 requires restoring the backup taken before the upgrade.

E31 — Keeping the books v0.15.0

In progress

A backup that restores everything: the .keshbackup file carries the supporting documents and frozen invoice PDFs, and Kesh writes a complete backup on its own, at a regular interval set in the interface (#503). Then a dry run of a year-end closing in the administration manual, carried out on a copy. Also: an E2E smoke test in CI (#577) and Rust 1.97.1.
